Privacy

Draft for review by the project guide · last updated 24 October 2026

In short: this website does not ask who you are and does not use cookies or analytics. If you send a photo report, we keep the photo (with faces blurred), the place you confirm, the date, the problem and your note, and never your name, phone number or email. A few outside services (listed below) see a normal web request from your browser when you use it.

What we collect

When you look at the website: nothing. There are no accounts for visitors, no cookies, no analytics or tracking scripts, and no advertising.

When you send a photo report (the "Report a spot" page): see "Photo reports" below. When you use "Report this photo" on a resident report: only the report's reference and the reason you choose; nothing you type.

Your location

The map never asks for your device's location. On the report page, your browser asks for it only if you tap "Use my location", and only with your permission. It is used to place the pin on the map and to check that the pin is near where you are; it is not stored. Only the pin you confirm is stored with your report.

The complaint helper

When the complaint helper is added (planned for November 2026), it will build the message text inside your browser. Nothing you type is sent to us. If you choose to open WhatsApp or X with the message, that app receives it under its own privacy policy, and you decide whether to send it. We never send complaints on your behalf.

Outside services your browser talks to

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website. Like any web host, it receives your IP address and browser details when you load a page, and may keep them in server logs. See the GitHub privacy statement.
  • OpenStreetMap supplies the map images. Your browser downloads them directly from OpenStreetMap's servers, which receive your IP address and the area of the map you are viewing. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.
  • Google Maps is used only if you tap "Open in Google Maps" or "Get directions" on a hotspot. That opens Google Maps in a new tab, under the Google privacy policy. Nothing from Google loads on this website before you tap.
  • Supabase (servers in Mumbai, India) holds resident photo reports. Your browser reads the approved reports from it when you open the map or the report page, and sends your report or "Report this photo" to it. Like any server, it receives your IP address. We use the address only in scrambled form (a keyed code that changes every day) to limit spam, and delete it after 2 days. See the Supabase privacy policy.
  • Links to other websites (news reports, government pages, PMC and MPCB accounts) open those sites, which have their own privacy policies.

The website loads no other outside scripts or fonts. The map library (Leaflet) and the tools the report page uses (face blurring, reading the location printed on a photo, and a small spam puzzle that needs no cookies) are served from this website itself.

People in our data

  • Photos: we do not publish photos that show people's faces. Faces and vehicle number plates are blurred before a photo is published.
  • Officials: we list public office addresses, office phone numbers and official social media accounts only, never personal phone numbers. Elected representatives are listed by name and office, taken from official results.
  • Team members are named only as the team that built the project.

Photo reports

  • What we keep: your photo after your phone has blurred the faces it found and redrawn it (which removes hidden data such as the phone model and GPS tags), the place you confirm on the map, when the photo was taken, the problem you choose and your note.
  • What we never keep: your name, phone number, email, the original file from your phone, or your device's location.
  • Before anything is public: every report is private until a team member checks it. The reviewer blurs anything private the phone missed (faces, number plates) and removes names, numbers or emails from the note. Only that cleaned copy is ever published.
  • After the decision: the photo you sent is deleted as soon as a reviewer approves or rejects the report. If the team needs to visit the spot first, it stays private until they decide.
  • What the public sees: the cleaned photo, the place, the date, the problem, your note (as edited by the reviewer) and the report's reference. Approved reports are labelled "not verified by the team".
  • Report this photo: anyone can ask for a published photo to be checked again. It is taken off the site at once and goes back to a reviewer.
  • Spam limits: your scrambled connection address (see Supabase above) is kept for 2 days to limit how many reports or flags come from one connection.

Corrections and removal requests

If a published photo shows you, your vehicle or your home, use "Report this photo" on it: it comes off the site at once. For anything else about you that you want corrected or removed, open an issue on our GitHub page (do not include private details there) or contact the team through our project guide at the college. We aim to reply within 7 days.
